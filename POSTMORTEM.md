# Post-mortem: the bottleneck is target generation, not search

*2026-10-20. Written after the first full day of automated runs. Read this before
contributing — it is the main thing we have learned, and it is a negative result.*

## The numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| Targets combed in one day | 27 (plus 27 in a parallel session) |
| Real survivors | **1** |
| Weak survivors kept with caveats | 2 |
| Verified leads refuted | ~60 |
| Scouts that correctly returned **zero** candidates | 16 |
| **Targets carrying a factually FALSE premise** | **18 of 27** |

The search stage works. The verification stage works — arguably too well, in that it
spent its budget disproving claims that should never have been queued. What failed is the
step before either of them: **writing the target.**

## What a false premise looks like

Targets were generated by an automated run that wrote confident claims and did not check
them. The scouts then burned their entire budget disproving their own brief.

1. **"Nobody has measured X."** Someone measured exactly X. Dithionite on selenate in FGD
   water → Ecolab holds US11535537B2. The legacy-phosphorus batch-and-pot experiment →
   SARE GNE15-111 ran it on the very soils the notes named.
2. **"These two literatures never cite each other."** The most on-point modern review
   cites the old corpus by name, repeatedly. One thesis reference list falsified **five**
   premises at once, including a 1974 *Desalination* paper whose title literally names the
   endpoint.
3. **The target misquotes its own "verified live" source.** `sulfate-below-gypsum` cited
   SAIMM 121(10):519-530 for a claim that paper's own Table II contradicts.
4. **A settled question stated as open.** The KAN smoothness/exactness question is settled
   negatively (Vitushkin/Henkin; Actor & Knepley).
5. **The prescribed experiment is physically incoherent.** The Zn-Cr methanol catalyst was
   to be tested at 30-80 bar / 220-300 °C. It needs 250-350 bar and 320-400 °C to have any
   rate or favourable equilibrium at all.
6. **The cross-field bridge needs the opposite sign.** Photographic deep-electron-trap
   dopants starve silver reduction; perovskite halide damage is *hole* oxidation of iodide.

## The fixes

**At generation time** — targets now carry a written `premises_verified` field quoting
what was searched and what the most on-point modern reference list actually contains, plus
a `physical_coherence_check`. The first run using this killed two candidate targets *before*
queuing them rather than after combing them.

Prefer targets whose gap has a **mechanistic** reason — a scope condition changed, a
documented commercial abandonment, a vocabulary split for the same physical quantity —
over purely bibliographic gaps. Every decisive kill came from mechanism or arithmetic.
None came from citation counting.

**In the scout prompt**, four additions measurably changed behaviour:
- *Source fidelity*: read the sentences around the one you want; if a reference in your own
  list names your endpoint, open it.
- *Broad co-occurrence*: search the bare concept against the bare target system with no
  application-domain words.
- *Check the target's premises first.*
- *Do not report a pure citation-counting gap with no mechanistic reason — that is padding.*

With all four in place, a scout found a genuine citation gap (Vitushkin's
variation-of-order-k invariant, unused in KAN rate proofs) and **declined to report it as
padding.** That is the behaviour to aim for.

## Other failure modes worth knowing

- **Citation misattribution was the most common hard failure.** Scouts reconstruct
  abstracts from OpenAlex inverted indexes, never see the author line, and attach the wrong
  one. One cited paper turned out to be a *harm* report arguing the opposite of the lead.
  Require first author and journal from a record that actually serves them.
- **"Zero hits in Europe PMC" does not mean unexplored.** One lead died to a 1996
  conference proceedings volume by the *same lab* that wrote both cited sources, which had
  already run the exact proposed experiment. Proceedings, theses and grey literature are
  invisible to these sweeps.
- **`read_full_text` was FALSE on all 22 candidates in one arm.** Abstract-level work is
  where the bad leads live.

## What this means if you are joining

Expect your ledger to be mostly rejections. That is the routine working correctly, not a
sign you set it up wrong. The interesting output of a day's combing may be a single lead
plus a list of things that are not true — and the list of things that are not true is
genuinely worth publishing, because it stops the next person re-walking it.

Do not pad. A padded ledger makes the pool worthless to everyone in it.

## Update 2026-10-21: a blocked check is a refutation, not a caveat

LC-0001 (Erdos #634) was promoted to the public ledger and is **wrong**. Its premise was
that erdosproblems.com had not recorded the prime case as settled. The proof-claims thread
had carried exactly that result since 2026-07-24, three months before our sweep.

Our verifier was Cloudflare-blocked (403) from that page. It recorded the block as a caveat,
scored the lead 5, and we published. Loading the page in a real browser took under a minute
and refuted it outright.

**This is the second occurrence.** The pilot's Erdos #1221 died identically: a scout asserted
the page omitted a preprint; the page already listed it.

**The rule:** no lead may assert that a tracker, review or database omits something unless
the omission was verified by actually loading the relevant page. If the page cannot be
reached, the lead is REFUTED, not caveated. Fetch-level 403s are often solvable by rendering
in a real browser; try that before giving up.

**A second trap this exposed:** the #634 *comments* thread is genuinely empty. The content
was under *proof claims*, a separate tab. Checking one surface of a tracker and reporting the
other as absent is not a check. Enumerate every surface a result could be recorded on.
